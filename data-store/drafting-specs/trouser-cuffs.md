# Trouser Cuffs (Turn-Ups) — The Anchor of the Trouser

_Authored by the brand owner, 2026-08-01. Verbatim. This document is LAW for the options it names; illustrations and other descriptions aid, not direct._

---

A trouser cuff is not simply a folded hem.

It is a counterweight.

Tailors use cuffs to alter how fabric hangs, where the eye stops, and how the leg visually terminates above the shoe.

Think of the trouser leg as a hanging curtain.

The cuff becomes the weighted bar at the bottom.

## CUFFS AND BODY PROPORTIONS

### Short Men

Large cuffs can shorten visual leg length.

Best choices:

    No Cuff
    3.2cm
    3.8cm

Effect: Maintains verticality.

### Tall Men

Can absorb larger cuffs easily.

Best choices:

    4.4cm
    5.1cm

Effect: Adds visual balance.

### Broad Shoulders

Large cuffs help counterbalance the upper body.

Effect: Creates symmetry.

### Slim Builds

Smaller cuffs often look cleaner.

Effect: Avoids excessive visual weight.

## HOW FABRIC CHANGES THE CUFF

### Lightweight Fabric — 220g-260g

Appearance: Cuff creates needed weight.

Effect: Improves drape dramatically.

### Heavy Fabric — 320g-450g

Appearance: Cuff becomes very substantial.

Effect: Creates powerful lower-leg structure.

## THE FLORENTINE BESPOKE LOOK

The classic Pitti Uomo / Florentine trouser usually combines:

    4.5-5.0cm waistband
    Extended tab
    Double reverse pleats
    Side adjusters
    No belt loops
    Wide or tapered leg
    4.4cm cuff
    Pressed crease

### Visual Result

The trousers appear suspended from the waist rather than wrapped around it.

The pleats create flowing volume.

The cuff catches and controls that volume.

### Mental Picture

Like a theater curtain hanging from a marble cornice, weighted perfectly at the bottom so every fold falls exactly where it should.
