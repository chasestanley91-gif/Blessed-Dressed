# Shirt Cuffs — Tailoring Geometry & Visual Engineering Guide

_Authored by the brand owner, 2026-08-01. Verbatim. This document is LAW for the options it names; illustrations and other descriptions aid, not direct._

---

A cuff is not merely a sleeve closure. It is the terminal architecture of the shirt. Every collar frames the face, but every cuff frames the hand.

A cuff controls:

    Wrist Width
    Hand Emphasis
    Sleeve Length Balance
    Jacket Sleeve Presentation
    Jewelry Interaction
    Formality Level
    Visual Weight Distribution

In a properly tailored shirt, approximately:

    1.0cm - 1.5cm

of cuff should remain visible beneath the jacket sleeve.

The cuff should act as a visual anchor balancing the collar.

## STANDARD SQUARE CUFF
`cuff-square`

### Geometric Construction

The outer closure edge forms a perfect rectangle.

     ________
    |        |
    |        |
    |________|

All corners terminate at:

    90°

angles.

No radius.

No taper.

No curvature.

### Drafting Geometry

Typical dimensions:

    Finished Height:
    6.0-7.0cm

    Corner Radius:
    0cm

    Closure Overlap:
    3.0cm

### Visual Appearance

Every line terminates decisively.

The cuff appears engineered.

The eye reads structure and order.

### Optical Illusion

✅ Stronger wrists

✅ Broader hand appearance

✅ More authority

✅ More business-oriented

### Visual Picture

Like the corner of an expensive architectural blueprint.

### Style Character
Business
Executive
Traditional

---

In true bespoke styling, the cuff should visually echo the collar. A soft rounded cutaway collar pairs naturally with a rounded cuff, while a high-stand sharp-corner spread collar is most harmonious with a square or mitered cuff. The collar frames the face, the cuff frames the hands, and together they create the visual rhythm of the shirt.
