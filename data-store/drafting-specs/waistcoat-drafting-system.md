# Waistcoat Drafting System — True Pattern Architecture

_Authored by the brand owner, 2026-08-01. Verbatim. This document is LAW for the options it names; illustrations and other descriptions aid, not direct._

---

Now we're getting into true pattern architecture.

A vest (waistcoat) is much less forgiving than a jacket because it has:

No sleeves to hide balance errors.
No front overlap to disguise distortion.
No shoulder padding to correct posture.
No skirt drape to mask fit problems.

A well-drafted vest is almost entirely a geometry exercise.

## MASTER WAISTCOAT BLOCK

Variables:

    C  = Chest Circumference
    W  = Waist Circumference
    S  = Seat Circumference

    BL = Back Length

    SH = Shoulder Width

    NC = Neck Circumference

    AH = Armhole Depth

    E  = Ease

Formal waistcoat ease:

    Chest Ease

    =
    2cm to 5cm

    Waist Ease

    =
    0cm to 3cm

Much smaller than jacket ease.

## FRONT WAISTCOAT BALANCE

A vest hangs from:

    Shoulders
    Center Front
    Armhole

The front balance is controlled by:

    Neck Point
    to
    Front Waist

Formula:

    Front Length

    =
    Back Length

    +
    1.0cm to 2.5cm

to compensate for chest projection.

## ARMHOLE GEOMETRY

Waistcoat armholes are normally higher than jacket armholes.

Draft:

    AH

    =
    Chest ÷ 4

    +
    0.5cm

Example:

    Chest = 100

    100 ÷ 4

    =
    25cm

    + .5

    =
    25.5cm

Compared to a jacket:

    27cm

### WHY

The vest must sit under the jacket.

A low armhole produces bunching.

## WAIST SUPPRESSION FORMULA

For a tailored waistcoat:

    Suppression

    =

    Chest Width
    -
    Waist Width

Typical:

    Chest = 100

    Waist = 88

    Difference

    = 12cm

Distribute:

    2cm side seam

    2cm front seam

    2cm dart

    Per side

Total:

    12cm
