## The strategy in one page

**The goal is not "get fabric." It is to end the quarter with three things:**

1. **A standing programme** — 2–3 mills that will sell you the everyday cloth at
   $10–16/yd, reliably, in the same quality twelve months from now.
2. **A premium book** — 2–3 merchant accounts whose bunches you can show a customer,
   where you buy a single length only when a customer has already paid for it.
3. **A hunting list** — jobbers you check monthly for high-Super closeouts at
   $15–35/yd, bought opportunistically and never promised to anyone twice.

Those are three different behaviours and the plan below runs them as three different
waves. The most common way a small maker wastes a year is trying to make one supplier
serve all three.

**Sequencing logic.** Open the free, no-gate accounts first — they cost nothing, they
arrive fastest, and holding two or three real trade accounts is itself the credential
that makes the *next* tier take you seriously. Run price discovery with Asian mills in
parallel, because that is the long pole: quote-only, timezone-lagged, and the answer
you most need. Save the premium merchants for last, when you have an account history,
a website and a resale certificate to point at.

**The one number to protect.** Never state your target price to any vendor. Ask what
their price is. Your $15/yd ceiling is scoring information, not negotiating information —
the moment a mill knows it, it becomes their anchor and your quotes stop being real.

## Step 0 — Before a single email goes out

Merchants gate on credibility, not money. Missing paperwork is the single most common
reason a bunch request is quietly ignored. Assemble this first; it takes an afternoon.

- [ ] **Business email on your own domain.** `dustin@blesseddressed.com` — never Gmail.
      This alone changes reply rates with trade desks.
- [ ] **EIN** and **state resale certificate** — scanned, ready to attach on request.
      Nearly every US and UK merchant asks; several will not ship bunches without it.
- [ ] **A business address** that is not obviously residential.
- [ ] **A live website** with a real "About" page. Merchants look. It does not need a
      shop — it needs to look like a going concern.
- [ ] **A one-paragraph company description** you reuse verbatim everywhere, so your
      story is identical across 57 conversations.
- [ ] **Re-verify Wave 2 contacts.** Wave 2 emails and phone numbers came through the
      search index, not a live page fetch (see the ledger's evidence note). Open each
      supplier's own contact page and confirm before sending. ~30 minutes, and it
      prevents a batch of bounces that would look like rejection.
- [ ] **A courier budget of ~$200.** Chinese mills send free cloth but bill courier at
      $25–40, refunded against a first order. Six mills is ~$180 to build a real
      budget swatch book. Cheapest money you will spend this quarter.

## The eight questions

Every email asks the same eight things. Same order, every time, so replies are
comparable in a spreadsheet instead of a pile of prose.

1. How does a US business open a trade account, and what documentation do you need?
2. Are swatch cards / bunches available, on what terms — and is any cost reimbursable?
3. What is your **minimum cut per order**? Can you cut a single suit length (3.2 m) or
   shirt length (2 m)?
4. Indicative **price per metre** at the specs below, and your price breaks.
5. Lead time and typical dispatch.
6. Shipping to the US — incoterm, and who carries duty?
7. Is any **minimum annual purchase** attached to holding your books?
8. **Continuity** — can I reorder this exact cloth in twelve months, or is it seasonal?

Question 8 is the one small makers forget and it is the one that hurts. A closeout
house can quote you $8/yd and be unable to sell you the same cloth twice; that is fine
for a hunting budget and fatal for a house cloth a customer will want again.

## The specs to quote

Send these verbatim. Vague RFQs get vague quotes.

**Suiting — everyday programme**
> All-wool worsted and wool-rich blends, 250–290 gsm, 150 cm usable width, piece-dyed
> and yarn-dyed. Plain weave, twill, and high-twist / tropical constructions.
> Please quote separately for all-wool and for blends, and state the exact composition.

**Suiting — tropical / high twist**
> High-twist worsted, 220–260 gsm, open plain weave, Super 110s–130s.
> Fresco-type dry handle. State Super count and micron.

**Suiting — premium**
> Super 130s / 150s / 180s all-wool worsted, 240–280 gsm.
> Quote per metre and state minimum cut. Note any mill-archive or remnant lots.

**Shirting — cotton**
> 100% cotton, 110–140 gsm, 150 cm width, 2-ply yarns (80s/2, 100s/2, 120s/2).
> Poplin, twill, oxford and royal oxford. Yarn-dyed and piece-dyed.

**Shirting — stretch**
> Woven cotton/elastane, **96/4 or 97/3**, 110–140 gsm, 150 cm width, weft stretch.
> Poplin and twill. *(Not 92/8 — that is a knit ratio. See the ledger.)*

Always add: **"Please confirm whether the Super count is certified and state the
micron."** Super numbering is unregulated in much of Asia and this question separates
the mills that measure from the ones that market.

## Rules of engagement

- **Never state your target price.** Not once, not as a "range", not as a hint.
- **Never share tech packs, patterns, construction specs or customer data** with any
  mill flagged with a garment arm — Raymond, Sunshine, Nanshan, Lianfa, Luthai,
  Vardhman, OCM, Altınyıldız, Wooltex, Zegna, Loro Piana, Siyaram/Donear.
  Buy cloth. Share nothing else.
- **One named human per email.** "Dear Sir/Madam" to a general inbox is the lowest
  reply rate in sourcing.
- **Always close on a specific next step** — a card, a quote, an account form. Never
  "let me know."
- **Convert every quote to $/yd before comparing** ($/m ÷ 1.0936). Metre prices look
  9% cheaper than they are.
- **Log every send in the tracker the same day.** An untracked outreach campaign
  becomes a guess within two weeks.

## Cadence

| Day | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Wave A + Wave B sent. Logged in tracker. |
| 2 | Wave C sent. |
| 3 | Wave D sent. |
| 4 | **Bump 1** on all non-responders — three lines, re-ask the single most important question. |
| 10 | **Bump 2** — add something new: a specific cloth reference, or your now-open trade accounts. |
| 14 | Wave A accounts should be live. Score Wave B quotes. Wave E goes out. |
| 30 | **Reactivation** on everyone still silent, with genuinely new information only. |
| 30+ | Three touches and stop. Park them; revisit at a trade show. |

Silence after three touches is an answer. Small makers burn months re-emailing mills
that were never going to sell to them, while the four that replied go unworked.

## Scoring a reply

Five gates. A supplier that fails 1 or 2 is not a supplier for you, whatever the price.

1. **Will they sell you a single length?** (3.2 m suiting / 2 m shirting) — pass/fail
2. **Is the landed $/yd inside band?** — $16 everyday, $30 ceiling premium
3. **Can you reorder it in twelve months?** — pass/fail
4. **Swatch terms** — free / refundable / paid
5. **Free and clear** — do they have a garment arm, and does it change their behaviour?

Then the tiebreakers: lead time, who carries duty, quality of the reply itself. A mill
that answers all eight questions in order is telling you something real about how it
will handle your orders.

## What success looks like at 90 days

- 3+ live trade accounts, at least one with no minimum (Standeven is the likeliest).
- A real per-metre number for all-wool worsted from at least 2 Asian mills — closing
  the ledger's biggest gap.
- A physical swatch book covering budget through premium.
- One cloth identified as a **house cloth**: in band, reorderable, from a supplier who
  will cut single lengths.
- A monthly closeout check running at Metro, Fabric Mart and B. Black for the
  high-Super hunt.
