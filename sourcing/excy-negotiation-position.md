# EXCY — Negotiation Position
Prepared 2026-08-28. Companion to `excy-price-analysis.md`.

## Objective

Two changes, asked together in one message:

1. **Structural** — replace the per-option price list with a blanket make-up rate per garment.
2. **Commercial** — approximately 7% reduction against the à la carte total for our specification.

Open at 10%. Expect to land at 6–8%.

## Why the structure is the real ask

The price lists Fukuda-san sent are a **dealer list** — built for a retail tailor shop
that marks each option up to its own customer. Blessed & Dressed runs the inverse model:
every construction and design option is included, and the client pays one price for the
garment they design. Three years of offering unlimited customisation at no upcharge is the
core differentiator.

Under a per-option list, every line item becomes an absorbed cost rather than a passed-on
one. The rate is not the problem; the architecture is.

A blanket rate is also **cheaper for the factory to administer** — no line-item counting at
order entry, fewer specification errors, one number per garment on the invoice. Frame it as
mutual, because it is.

## Position on double-breasted vs single-breasted

Do **not** argue that DB should cost the same as SB. It genuinely costs more to make: more
fabric handling, more buttonholes, the wrap, the internal anchor button, more pressing.
¥2,070 on a ¥27,800 base is ~7.4%, which is roughly the real difference. Arguing otherwise
signals to a Japanese factory that we do not understand construction, and costs credibility
we need for the pattern conversation.

The correct ask is **one blended jacket rate regardless of front style**. Same outcome —
DB stops being a line item — without the credibility cost.

## Tier boundaries — define precisely or the à la carte creep returns

**Included in the blanket** (machine-executed): pocket and vent styles, ticket pockets,
working sleeve buttons, buttonhole and thread colours including contrast, lining and button
selection, sleeve lining, sweat stop, bar tacks, waistband and pleat styles, watch pockets,
V-slit, centre crease, turn-ups, shirt collar and cuff shapes, contrast inner facings, bias
cutting, split yokes, and any pattern-specific ("irregular") instructions.

**Passthrough — hand-work tier**, billed to the client:
- AMF pick stitching, full or partial
- Hand-sewn buttons ("=" stitch)
- Individual hand-made parts
- Hand-stitched shirt collar and cuff

**Passthrough — service tier**, billed to the client:
- Basting / 仮縫い (fitting garment)
- Expedited delivery (shirt one-week, one-day)

## Volume tiers

Attach the discount to volume — that is what makes it grantable rather than merely
requested. Fukuda-san already thinks in these terms; his proprietary-pattern guideline was
~600 garments/year.

Proposed: 100 / 250 / 500 garments per year.

**These must be numbers we can actually hit.** A missed commitment resets the rate and
costs more credibility than the discount is worth. Confirm before sending.

## Sequencing — do not get this wrong

The correction-limit question is unresolved. If the answer is that our body type requires
the proprietary pattern system, every rate above is priced for the wrong service.

- Ask the pattern question and the commercial restructure **in the same message**. Do not
  spend two rounds.
- Do **not** sign a volume commitment before a basted first article proves the pattern.
- Do **not** disclose our retail pricing or landed-cost targets. A percentage against their
  own list is normal commercial negotiation; our margins are not their business.
